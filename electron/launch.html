Let TSS control your teleprompter

TSS drives your teleprompter app for you — typing and clicking into it on every machine, in sync. macOS protects you by asking permission before any app can send keystrokes or mouse to another, so there's a one-time setup. It's quick, and nothing ever leaves your Mac.

  1. 1

    Open it the first time

    TSS isn't signed with a paid Apple certificate, so the first launch macOS may call it an "unidentified developer." In Applications, right-click (or Control-click) Teleprompter Sync System → Open → Open. If it's still blocked on newer macOS, open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway. You only do this once.

  2. 2

    Allow Accessibility

    checking…

    This is the one TSS really needs — it's what lets the app send keystrokes and mouse to your teleprompter. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility, then switch on Teleprompter Sync System. If it's already listed but off, toggle it on (or remove and re-add it after an update).

  3. 3

    Say "OK" to Automation if asked

    The first time TSS nudges your teleprompter app, macOS may pop up "Teleprompter Sync System wants to control…". Click OK. You can change it anytime under Privacy & Security → Automation.

Why this is safe: these permissions only let TSS control the apps you choose, over your local network. No cloud, no account, nothing recorded — the access simply lets one app on your Mac drive another, which is exactly how TSS keeps your rigs in sync.
